01 / THE CHALLENGE

Financial analysis lived outside the product.

The TMS already handled shipment requests, acceptance and trips. But customers and transporters still relied on Excel to understand their finances.

They manually updated shipments and trips, calculated totals and tracked freight, detention, loading, unloading and penalties. Reports became difficult to maintain, easy to make inconsistent, and quickly outdated.

02 / TWO USERS

One operational system. Two financial questions.

CUSTOMER

Where is my money going?

Understand shipment spend, cost drivers, transporter performance and the gap between expected and actual cost.

TRANSPORTER

Where is my money coming from?

Understand revenue by route and client, cost leakage and the difference between expected and actual earnings.

04 / RESEARCH

Understanding how finance worked in the real operation.

I spoke with logistics and supply-chain POCs from CEAT Tyres and ITC Tobacco, along with their transporters.

Existing Excel reports with dummy data helped me understand what teams tracked, how they calculated costs, how information was communicated across organisational levels, and which questions mattered to each persona.

What are we spending? Overall expense, routes, transporters and cost components.
Why are we spending it? Detention, penalties, loading, unloading and other drivers.
Is the cost changing? Trends, averages and shipment-level comparisons.
How different is reality from expectation? Expected vs actual expense and variance.

06 / KPI DESIGN

Make the number explainable.

Overall Shipment Expense was designed as a number users could break down into the operational events that created it.

OVERALL SHIPMENT EXPENSE
Freight Rate + Loading Charges + Unloading Charges + Detention Charges − Late Delivery Penalty
FREIGHT CHARGES
Contract base rate × quantity used in the shipment
DETENTION COST
Contract cost per hour × detention hours

08 / INSIGHT → SOURCE

Let users move from an insight to the record behind it.

Charts answer the high-level question. Tables let users investigate.

Shipment-level data was presented in a table so users could inspect individual costs and components, with a direct path back to the shipment request for the complete operational context.

10 / WHAT I LEARNED

Applying systems thinking more rigorously.

I already understood the value of thinking in systems, but this project helped me apply it more rigorously to a complex product.

A KPI was never just a number on a dashboard. It connected to a business question, a formula, multiple data points, and the operational workflows where those data points were generated.

I got better at seeing the dots, understanding how they connect, and designing those connections into a smarter product.
